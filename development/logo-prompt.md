# EYENET — logo design prompt

Paste the block below into Claude (design) to start iterating on a logo.
Everything above the line is context for you; the prompt itself is under it.

---

Design a logo for **EYENET**, a forensic observation framework for analyzing
threat actors. It watches, records, and preserves evidence — it does not act.
Think a quiet, disciplined surveillance instrument built for a single operator,
not an enterprise SOC. The tagline is **"Don't look. Observe."**

**Name meaning:** EYENET = *eye* + *net*. An eye that observes; a net that
catches and retains. Lean into one or both — an aperture/iris, a mesh or grid,
a reticle/crosshair, a watching mark. Avoid the literal cliché eyeball; favor
something abstract, geometric, and a little clinical.

**Feel:** dark, forensic, restrained, precise. Terminal-grade, not playful.
Evidence-locker seriousness with a covert edge. No gloss, no gradients-for-show,
no cute mascots.

**Visual system it must live in (match this):**
- Background is near-black. The logo must read on black first.
- Primary accent: muted violet — `#743093` (fills) / `#a259c4` (bright text/lines).
- Reserved signal colors, use sparingly if at all: muted red `#c2453f`,
  muted amber `#c29a3f`.
- Typography around it is a mono + clean sans pairing; the wordmark should feel
  at home next to monospace, uppercase, wide letter-spacing.
- Mood: 3 colors on black, high restraint.

**Deliverables to explore:**
1. A standalone **mark/glyph** that works as a 16px favicon and app icon —
   legible as a single silhouette, no fine detail that dies when small.
2. A **wordmark** "EYENET" (uppercase, mono-adjacent), and the mark + wordmark
   lockup used in a top bar and on a login screen.
3. One **monochrome** version (single violet, and pure white) so it survives on
   any surface and in a court-document footer.

**Constraints:**
- Must work at 16px and at hero size.
- Vector, crisp geometry, minimal path count.
- No stock "AI eye" tropes, no circuit-board texture, no lens flare.

Give me 3–4 distinct directions first (rough), then I'll pick one to refine.
